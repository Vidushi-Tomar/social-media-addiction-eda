# Social Media Addiction Among Students: An Exploratory Data Analysis

An end-to-end EDA project investigating how social media usage relates to sleep, mental health, academic performance, and platform preference among students across countries.

## 📌 Project Overview

This project explores a student survey dataset covering social media usage, addiction scores, sleep patterns, mental health, and academic impact. The goal is to uncover patterns between platform choice, usage behavior, and student wellbeing using Python-based data analysis and visualization.

## 🎯 Key Questions Explored

- How does age, gender, and academic level relate to social media addiction?
- Which countries and platforms show the highest addiction levels?
- Is there a correlation between sleep hours and addiction scores?
- Does mental health correlate with addiction and social media conflicts?
- Which platforms are most commonly linked to academic disruption?
- How does platform preference vary by country?

## 🛠️ Tools & Libraries

- **Python**
- **pandas** – data cleaning & aggregation
- **numpy** – numerical operations
- **seaborn** & **matplotlib** – data visualization

## 🔍 Key Insights

- **Sleep & Addiction:** A strong negative correlation exists between sleep hours and addiction scores — less sleep, higher addiction.
- **Mental Health:** Students with poorer mental health scores show significantly higher addiction scores and are more prone to social-media-related conflicts.
- **Platform Impact:** Instagram, TikTok, and WhatsApp are most frequently cited as affecting academic performance, while Facebook and LinkedIn are seen as less disruptive.
- **Geography:** Platform preference varies notably by country — WhatsApp dominates in France and Switzerland, Instagram in Denmark, Turkey, and the USA, and TikTok in Canada and Spain.
- **Demographics:** Most surveyed students are 20 years old; undergraduates show higher addiction levels than graduate or high school students.
